\begin{resumo}[Abstract]
\begin{otherlanguage*}{english}
Digital public service evaluations can lose information about the point in the journey where a user encounters a barrier. This study aims to design, implement, and technically verify an evaluation system that links navigation signals to self-reported feedback about a particular step. The research follows Design Science Research in two cycles. In the first, Lean Inception guided product design, and a Figma prototype was used in eight formative sessions, with four participants per flow and click and time data recorded through Useberry. The pilot yielded observations about the interface and how participants understood the journey. In the second, a local Python prototype was developed with an API, SQLite, configurable operational rules, a feedback form, and an administrative dashboard. The A/B prototype records active time, exposure, and responses and aggregates flow-specific denominators in the dashboard. Preliminary verification exercised both flows through the dashboard. Across 1,000 synthetic sessions, the initial rule achieved 50.25\% balanced accuracy, close to the 50\% baseline of a rule with no ability to discriminate between labels. The simulation characterizes how the rules behave under controlled conditions and does not establish their ability to detect difficulties in real use.

\vspace{\onelineskip}
\noindent \textbf{Keywords:} digital public services; contextual feedback; service evaluation; user experience; design science research.
\end{otherlanguage*}
\end{resumo}
