\begin{resumo}[Abstract]
 \begin{otherlanguage*}{english}
This work presents the design and initial development of a contextual evaluation
system for digital public services, in the context of the digital transformation
of the Brazilian State and the need to make citizen experience evaluation more
useful for continuous improvement. The research problem concerns evaluations
displayed only at the end of the user journey, which do not always capture the
moment when users face difficulty, spend excessive time, repeat clicks, or
abandon the flow. The methodology combined bibliographic and normative review,
Design Science Research, incremental prototyping, and Lean Inception. A local
prototype was developed with an API, simulated public service pages, interaction
telemetry based on clicks and time, difficulty-detection rules, a feedback form,
and an administrative dashboard. Telemetry produces behavioral evidence and is
not treated as a measure of perceived quality; perceived quality depends on
user-declared feedback such as stars, attributes, NPS, and comments. The
prototype underwent a preliminary exploratory evaluation in a controlled
environment with eight participants divided between a final-form flow and a
contextual-feedback flow. The results cannot be statistically generalized and do
not demonstrate the superiority of either flow, but provide initial indications
about task completion, perceived ease, and comment specificity. The study
concludes that integrating behavioral and declared evidence is a technological
and investigative contribution that can support further studies, provided that
thresholds are calibrated, data collection respects privacy, and the comparative
hypothesis is evaluated with larger samples.

   \vspace{\onelineskip}

   \noindent
   \textbf{Key-words}: digital public services. contextual feedback. service
   evaluation. user experience. Design Science Research.
 \end{otherlanguage*}
\end{resumo}
